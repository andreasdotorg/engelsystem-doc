# Reports and Logs

The Engelsystem tracks various statistics and maintains logs of system activity. As a bureaucrat, you have access to some of this information for monitoring and troubleshooting.

## Activity Logs

The system logs significant actions - user registrations, shift signups, administrative changes. You can view these logs to understand what's been happening and troubleshoot issues.

Logs are useful for:

- Investigating problems ("when did this change happen?")
- Verifying administrative actions were completed
- Understanding patterns of activity during the event

Filter logs by time range, action type, or user to find what you're looking for. During busy events, logs can be extensive.

## User Statistics

You can view aggregate information about users:

- Total registered angels
- Number who have arrived
- Distribution across angel types
- Active vs. inactive accounts

This information helps understand the overall state of the volunteer workforce.

## Shift Coverage

While detailed shift management may require higher permissions, you can typically see:

- How many shifts are scheduled
- Current coverage levels
- Which areas might be understaffed

If you notice coverage problems, alert shift coordinators who can address them.

## Work Log Reports

Work logs track hours credited to angels. You can view:

- Individual user work logs (hours from shifts plus manual entries)
- Aggregate statistics on hours worked
- Goodie eligibility based on hours

These reports help ensure angels get credit for their work and help event organizers understand volunteer participation.

## Interpreting Data

Statistics can be misleading without context. A few considerations:

**Timing matters.** Coverage looks different at 3 AM than at 3 PM. Low numbers during night shifts might be normal.

**Not all work is tracked.** Some valuable contributions happen outside the shift system. Don't assume hours worked equals total contribution.

**Trends over time.** Compare against previous events if data is available. What looks abnormal for this event might be typical historically.

## What Reports Can't Tell You

Numbers don't capture everything. They won't tell you:

- Whether angels are having a good experience
- If certain shifts are particularly difficult
- What communication problems exist
- How well teams are coordinating

Talk to angels and supporters to understand the qualitative side of how things are going.

{{% notice tip %}}
If you notice interesting patterns in the data, share them with event organizers. Your observations might inform planning for future events.

## Privacy and Access

Report data often contains personal information. Handle it appropriately:

- Don't share detailed reports publicly
- Aggregate statistics are generally safer to discuss
- Some reports may be restricted to higher permission levels

If someone requests detailed data, check whether they have a legitimate need and appropriate authorization before sharing.
